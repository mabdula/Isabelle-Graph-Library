HOW ONE MEASURED RUN WAS LAUNCHED -- matroid intersection study
================================================================

Every number in Tables 25 to 28 of the paper is derived from runs launched
exactly as follows.  The benchmark programs in this package print the raw
line of one such run; everything around the program is described here so
that a single run can be repeated under the same conditions.

THE PROBLEM
  One instance file and one of the four problems.  matching and
  weighted_matching read <family>_n<N>_s<K>.asn; b_matching and
  weighted_b_matching read the .bmatch file of the same graph with the
  stamped capacities (instances/README.txt).  Every solver got the same file.
  Weights are the arc costs of the .asn file, maximised; capacities 1
  everywhere is ordinary matching.

THE COMMAND
  taskset -c <core> timeout -k 10 1200 <program> <problem> <instance>
  <program> is one of
    bench_sml, bench_scala, bench_go                 (this directory)
    competitors/sage_oracle/bench_sage_oracle        column "Sage (our oracle)"
    competitors/sage_plain/bench_sage_plain          column "Sage (plain)"
  The Scala program is launched through its wrapper, which runs
    java -Xmx16g -XX:ActiveProcessorCount=1 -XX:+UseSerialGC
  and makes the measured call on a thread with a 24576 MB stack
  (SOLVE_STACK_MB), after a warm-up of three solves of a fixed complete
  bipartite graph with 24 vertices a side (see code/scala/OUTPUT.txt).

REPEATS AND AGGREGATION
  ONE run per instance (no repeats).  Five seeds per size.  A table cell is
  the mean of the five cpu_ms values of that size, in seconds; for Table 25
  (matching and b-matching), where low-cost and two-cost share their graphs,
  the cell is the mean over the ten runs of both families.  The
  parenthesised ratio is the cell divided by the SML cell of the same size;
  (-) where the SML mean is 0 ms.  A superscript k/m marks a mean over the k
  of m instances that finished.

PINNING
  One run per L3 cache island, seven islands in use (cores 4, 8, 12, 16, 20,
  24, 28 of the 8 x 4-core islands of the machine), the eighth island
  (cores 0-3) reserved for everything that is not a measurement.  taskset is
  outermost so the timeout wrapper is confined to the run's core as well.
  No warm-up spin preceded the program.

TIMEOUT AND LADDER
  1200 s wall per run, then SIGKILL after 10 s.  A timeout is recorded as
  `timeout' with no cpu_ms; every larger size of that solver, problem and
  family is recorded `timeout_inferred' without being run (TO in the paper).
  The propagation never crosses a solver, a problem or a family.  The Scala
  program dying of "out of heap" (the dense geometric instances at -Xmx16g)
  is read the same way (OOM in the paper); this campaign still attempted the
  larger sizes and recorded them as errors, so those rows are measured where
  a later campaign would infer them.

THE GRID
  low-cost and two-cost: n = 32, 64, ..., 32768 (11 sizes), seeds 1-5.
  geometric:             n = 32, 64, ..., 4096 (8 sizes), seeds 1-5.
  All four problems, all five solvers, 3000 runs at most.  Every solver runs
  the whole ladder, small rungs included.

WHICH CAMPAIGN THE COLUMNS COME FROM
  All five columns: the single campaign of 2026-10-09 23:05 to 2026-10-10
  (campaign id 20261009_224301), raw rows in published_results/solver_csvs.zip
  (<solver>/<problem>/<family>.csv, 13 columns, 3000 rows; its
  conditions.txt is the campaign's own flags file).  Before the timed runs
  an agreement check ran the five solvers on two validation instances
  (val_n32_s1, val_n256_s1, the low-cost recipe at n = 32 and 256): all
  agreed on card for the unweighted problems and on weight for the weighted
  ones.

THE CLOCK
  CPU time (user + system) read immediately before the call into the solver
  and immediately on return: for the exports, the entry point and the
  forcing of the thunk it returns, with the instance already read into
  arrays; for Sage, the intersection call alone, with the two matroids
  already built (time.process_time).  Reading the instance, building the
  matroids, checking the answer and computing card and weight are all
  outside.  The Scala figure is the solver THREAD's clock (ThreadMXBean),
  which under the serial collector omits collection pauses; proc_ms beside
  it is the whole process.  cpu_ms is the only time the tables use.
